Knowledge is Power

Project Navigation:
Bios | Feasibility | Labs

1. Introduction

  • 1.1. Societal Problem: Young adults (ages 16–19) face severe, life-altering future derailment due to a lack of legal awareness regarding common laws.
  • 1.2. Needed Solution Characteristics: Engaging, accessible, and interactive educational tools that promote self-accountability.
  • 1.3. Product Introduction: Knowledge Is Power (KIP), a digital platform designed to teach essential laws to young adults, acting as an educational aid for parents and school administrators.

2. KIP Product Description

  • 2.1. Solution Overview: KIP utilizes a minimalist, game-based interface paired with real-world video assets to educate young adults on relevant legal boundaries.
  • 2.2. Goals and Objectives: To assess, improve, and reinforce legal knowledge among tech-savvy youth in 2026 through low-stakes, engaging interactivity.

2.1. Key Product Features and Capabilities

  • 2.1.1. Interactive Gamification: Uses succinct, timed, quirky games to assess current knowledge.
    • 2.1.1.1. "Burn the Toast" Game: Timed quiz on copyright law where final toast quality reflects correct answers.
    • 2.1.1.2. "Pick the Tomato" Game: Timed quiz on driving laws where crop ripeness reflects correct answers.
  • 2.1.2. Module Widgets: Integration of publicly available bodycam videos tied to specific legal topics.
  • 2.1.3. Low-Cost Data Collection: Tracks user engagement and topical interest choices seamlessly during use.

2.2. Major Components (Hardware/Software)

  • 2.2.1. Hardware Requirements: Standard consumer smartphones, tablets, and computers.
  • 2.2.2. System Architecture: Structured according to the CS 410 Major Functional Component Diagram (MFCD).
  • 2.2.3. Software to be Developed: Web-based application interface, interactive game engines, and video widget modules.

3. Identification of Case Study

  • 3.1. Primary Target Audience: Young adults aged 16–19 navigating high-risk legal areas (driving, school, alcohol consumption).
  • 3.2. Secondary Stakeholders: Parents and high school administrators seeking effective educational aids.
  • 3.3. Future Users: Juvenile diversion programs, community youth groups, and driving schools.
